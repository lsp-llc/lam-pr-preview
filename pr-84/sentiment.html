02 How to Read These Numbers

These charts use download and like counts from the HuggingFace Hub — but raw popularity needs a little interpretation before you act on it.

Downloads

Last-30-day pulls — a strong usage signal, but inflatable. CI pipelines, mirrors, and automated runs all count, so a spike isn't always human adoption.

Likes

Cumulative manual endorsements — a slower, more deliberate signal of approval than downloads. Harder to game, but it lags new releases.

Recency

Fresh releases spike then settle; long-stable models show steady numbers. A model at the top of “trending” hasn't necessarily proven itself yet.

Blind spots

Gated, private, and renamed repos undercount. A Hub 401/404 stays on this tracked list at last-known or zero — we do not invent a count. Likes are not a grade. Popularity ≠ quality or fit — the most-downloaded model may not run on your VRAM or suit your task.

Popularity is a starting point, not a verdict. To turn it into a pick that fits your hardware, run the Model Targeting System or the Stack Builder.

04 Using This Dashboard

This dashboard shows two numbers for every model it tracks: how many downloads the model has on the HuggingFace Hub, and how many likes it has collected there. Both come from a snapshot the site refreshes weekly, and the date of the snapshot in use is printed under the page title. Read them as a ranking of relative traction, not as a measure of quality.

What the dashboard shows

Two charts, drawn from the same weekly snapshot, plus a short dated movers strip when week-over-week ranks or counts actually moved. The first chart ranks the top ten by download count. The second ranks the top ten by likes and carries each model's download figure alongside, so you can see where the two signals disagree. Bar lengths are scaled against the leading model in each chart, which makes the shape a comparison rather than an absolute scale. Every model name opens that model's catalog card, so you can jump from the ranking to the local-run notes on this site.

What the signals mean and where they stop

Each figure is one reading taken on one date of HuggingFace's rolling 30-day download counter, so it reflects roughly the month of activity before the fetch date shown at the top of the page. Treat it as a relative signal for comparing models within that snapshot, not as a total you can add up or trend across weeks. Downloads count machine pulls as readily as human ones, so continuous integration runs, mirrors, and automated evaluation all inflate the figure. Likes are manual and deliberate, which makes them slower to move and harder to game, but they lag new releases badly. Gated, private, and renamed repositories undercount. And the snapshot covers the models this site tracks, not the whole Hub.

Why traction matters when picking a model

Popularity is not quality, but it is a fair proxy for how easy a model will be to live with. A widely used model has quantized builds ready to download, support in the runtimes people actually run, prompt templates someone has already debugged, and a search result waiting for whatever error you hit at midnight. A model almost nobody runs may be excellent, and you will still be the one finding its rough edges. That is worth knowing before you commit a weekend to it.

Methodology and corrections

How these signals are collected, how often they refresh, and how corrections get published are all set out on the about page, which also states what this site does not do: no scraped reviews, no star ratings, no paid placement. If a figure here looks wrong, that page explains where to send it.
