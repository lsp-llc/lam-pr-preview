Know what AI is worth in your business before you decide what to spend on it.

Most companies do not arrive with a finished brief. They arrive knowing AI is going to change how they operate, without knowing which part of the business it reaches first. That is a reasonable place to start. This document is written for it.

What follows is the range of work we do. Point at what looks relevant, ask about what is unclear, and rule out the rest. Nothing here is priced. Scope comes first.

Workflow & Process Engineering

Redesign of the processes people run, with automation only where it justifies the effort. Human review stays where judgment belongs.

Typically  Proposal and RFP generation, document intake, support triage, CRM and record updates.

Data & Database Engineering

The records, documents, and retrieval layers AI depends on. This is where most tools fail, well before anyone writes a prompt.

Typically  Consolidating scattered records, building a searchable knowledge base, re-establishing a source of truth.

Identity, Access & AI Security

What an AI system is allowed to do and what it is allowed to reach, plus the exposure your current controls were never written to cover.

Typically  Service identities and least privilege, pre-deployment review, prompt injection and data-leakage testing.

Content Systems

The company's content built out at volume, with its voice intact. We build the system and the review standard, not the drafts.

Typically  Product and service content, sales collateral, technical documentation, internal knowledge.

AI-Native Education for Staff

Role-based literacy so people can use these tools competently and supervise the output. The capability stays in-house after we step back.

Typically  Department workshops, supervision and review standards, onboarding for new hires.

Executive & Board Briefings

Strategic-level education for the people who approve the budget and carry the risk. What to fund, what to defer, and what to ask before approving either.

Typically  Leadership sessions, board briefings, investment and risk framing.

Competitive & Market Analysis

How AI is changing your business and the businesses you compete with. What they have adopted, what it earned them, and where that leaves you.

Typically  Sector deep dive, competitor capability review, positioning and differentiation.

Horizon & Pace of Change

Where this is heading over your planning window and at what speed, so you can tell what is worth building now and what to wait on.

Typically  Planning-horizon briefings, build-versus-wait calls, annual strategy refresh.

How scope sets price

There is no price list.

Two companies can ask for the same thing and need entirely different work. Cost is driven by the number of people affected, the condition of the data, the systems that have to connect, and the regulatory exposure. The label on the request matters far less. Quoting before we understand those would be pricing someone else's problem.

So we scope first. A short intake and a scoping call establish what the work actually involves, and the price follows from that. If the honest answer is that you should not build anything yet, we will tell you that instead.

The arc of an engagement

Most clients enter in the middle. You do not have to start at the beginning, and you do not have to commit to the whole arc to begin.

Our method

We organize governance around the NIST AI Risk Management Framework — Govern, Map, Measure, Manage — and align with ISO/IEC 42001 principles. This is our discipline, not a certification we hold.

Your data

We work inside your environment and accounts where possible, scope access to what an engagement needs, and document where data goes and who can reach it. We identify the privacy tradeoffs of third-party models before you adopt them.

Who does the work

Backgrounds in military operations and logistics, information security (including CISSP certification), and finance. The recommendation and the implementation come from the same hands.
