Which local coding LLM runs on 8 GB of VRAM?

On 8 GB, the Artificial Analysis listing that fits is Qwen3.5 4B at Intelligence Index 20*, marked estimated on both the leaderboard and the model page. A second 4B row on the same table is 16*[1][2]. The same family card also covers the 9B, which this fetch of the leaderboard lists at 22[1].

This site's quantization guide prices Q4_K_M at 4.8 bits per weight, roughly 0.6 GB per billion parameters[5]. A 4B is 4 × 0.6 = 2.4 GB of weights: a calculation. The catalog family band is 3 to 18 GB across the 4B / 9B / 27B stack[4]. The 4B carries a vision encoder (Hub pipeline tag image-text-to-text), so leave room beyond the weight figure. A 9B at the same rule is 5.4 GB of weights and still an 8 GB-class load at short context.

Qwen3.5 4B. Alibaba, Apache 2.0, 262K native context on the catalog card and on AA[2][4]. AA's model page lists reasoning, text / image / video input, 4.7B total parameters, and "20 (estimated)" with independent evaluation forthcoming[2]. Hub reported 7,782,618 downloads and 843 likes for Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B on 2026-08-25[6].

Qwen3 8B and StarCoder2 3B / 7B remain catalog rows for this card. This fetch of the Intelligence Index table did not list them, so there is no AA score here[1]. Catalog: Qwen3 8B at 6 to 7 GB, Apache 2.0, 128K; StarCoder2 at 3 to 5 GB, 16K, BigCode OpenRAIL-M[4]. Hub on 2026-08-25: Qwen3-8B 14,619,976 downloads and 1,319 likes; StarCoder2-3B 142,378 / 221; StarCoder2-7B 39,317 / 222[6].

What is the best local LLM for coding in 2026?

On a single 24 GB card, the Artificial Analysis map points at Qwen3.8 27B (xhigh). This fetch of the Intelligence Index table lists it at 52[1][2]. Catalog Q4 is about 14 GB of weights (27B × 0.5); occupancy is not on the Hub card[4]. That leaves KV room on 24 GB. The same table lists the medium setting at 44, low at 43, and the unqualified 27B row at 35[1].

The useful tradeoff is that 52. Thinking is on by default; reasoning_effort is xhigh / medium / low on the catalog card[4]. AA's model page calls the xhigh listing verbose (160M output tokens on the Index run) and slow relative to its size class[2]. You buy the 52 by spending thinking tokens and KV that a 24 GB card also needs for context.

Qwen3.8 27B. Alibaba, Apache 2.0, Hub pipeline tag image-text-to-text. Catalog native context 262,144, extensible to 1,000,000. AA's leaderboard row lists 256k; the model-page FAQ lists 260k[1][2][4]. AA dates the xhigh listing to a 14 August 2026 release and scores the composite across reasoning, knowledge, mathematics, and coding. SciCode and Terminal-Bench v2.1 are named components of Index v4.1.1; this fetch did not return per-eval numeric scores, so none are quoted[1][2]. Hub reported 2,945,415 downloads and 12,606 likes for Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B on 2026-08-25[6].

Qwen3.6 27B. Same 27B / ~14 GB Q4 class, Apache 2.0, also image-text-to-text[4]. AA lists the reasoning row at 38 and a second 27B row at 31[1][2]. Hub reported 6,270,956 downloads and 2,279 likes on 2026-08-25[6].

Qwen3-Coder 32B Dense (catalog about 20 GB at Q4, Apache 2.0, 256K) and GLM-4.7-Flash (catalog about 20 GB at Q4, MIT, 200K) stay on the catalog as 24 GB dedicated-coder rows[4]. Can you run a coding LLM on a 16 GB RAM laptop?

Yes, if you keep the model at 8B or below and accept slower generation. A 16 GB laptop with no discrete GPU runs the model in system RAM on the CPU, and this site's quantization guide puts each offloaded layer at roughly 3 to 10 times slower than on a GPU[5]. A 16 GB discrete card is a much better situation.

On 16 GB of system RAM. Budget for the operating system first. Qwen3.5 4B at about 2.4 GB of Q4 weights is the AA-mapped load for this envelope[1][5]. A 14B at Q4 is about 8.4 GB, or roughly 10.7 GB as the catalog lists Qwen3 14B[4][5]: it fits, and leaves little for context or for anything else the laptop is doing. The VRAM guide covers the system-RAM and offload case in more detail.

On a 16 GB discrete GPU, the AA-listed dedicated coder that fits is Devstral Small 2 at Intelligence Index 18[1][2]. Qwen3.8 27B at ~14 GB of Q4 weights is a 24 GB job once KV and the vision encoder are in; this page keeps it there.

Which quantization should you use for a coding model on 8 GB?

Q4_K_M. It is this site's default for every VRAM figure it publishes, and the last level where structured code output holds up. Do not go below Q4 for coding, and do not reach for a bigger model at a lower quantization to compensate.

Two reasons, both from the quantization guide. Its sensitivity table rates structured code as highly sensitive, second only to math and logic, with syntax errors, wrong function signatures and broken JSON as the failures that appear below Q4[5]. And its rule of thumb is that a smaller model at a higher quantization usually beats a larger model at a lower one, precisely for coding[5].

On what Q4_K_M costs in quality, the quantization guide's practitioner-reported range is 95 to 97 percent of FP16 quality on most benchmarks[5]. The same guide applies a 1.2× to 1.5× multiplier over raw weight size for KV cache, activations and overhead. On an 8 GB card, use that buffer. The quantization guide has the full compression table and the per-task sensitivity breakdown, and the model catalog lists a Q4_K_M VRAM figure for every model it carries.

Quality on this page is Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.1.1. Fit is Hub license plus catalog or calculated VRAM. Named SWE figures stay on their original write-ups. Nothing here was measured on our hardware. Hub download dates are traction, not the reason a pick moved.

FAQ

Do I need a dedicated coding model, or will a general model do?

On 8 GB, AA lists Qwen3.5 4B at 20*, a general / vision 4B[1]. On 24 GB, Qwen3.8 27B (xhigh) at 52 sits above the dedicated coders AA lists at 18 to 21. Intelligence Index v4.1.1 includes SciCode and Terminal-Bench v2.1 plus six other evaluations, so a general 27B can outrank a dedicated coder on this map. Use Mistral's 68.0 percent SWE-bench Verified when you want a dedicated-coder SWE figure for Devstral Small 2[8].

Does a bigger context window help with code?

Only if you have the VRAM for it. This site's quantization guide prices an 8B-class KV cache at about 0.7 GB at 8K, 2.5 GB at 32K, and 10 GB at 128K, and a 70B-class cache at about 5.5 / 20 / 80 GB on the same steps[5]. A model advertising 256K context on a 24 GB card cannot use anywhere near that window. Qwen3.8 27B's xhigh 52 spends additional thinking tokens into that same budget.

Can I run these on an AMD card or an Apple Silicon Mac?

Broadly yes, with the same VRAM arithmetic. The catalog notes GLM-4.7-Flash running on RTX 3090 and 4090 class cards and on Apple M-series[4]. On unified-memory Macs the budget is shared with the system, so size down.

Where do DeepSeek V4 and Qwen3-Coder-Next sit relative to a 24 GB card?

Qwen3-Coder-Next is on the Intelligence Index at 21, with a catalog Q4 estimate of about 40 GB and serve examples on 2 to 4 GPUs[1][4]. V4 Pro appears as "DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (max)" at 53; catalog Q4 is about 800 GB of weights[1][4]. The 24 GB job on this map is Qwen3.8 27B (xhigh) at 52.
