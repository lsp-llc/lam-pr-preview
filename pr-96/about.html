About Local AI Models

Local AI Models is a reference and toolset for engineers who run open-weight AI models on their own hardware.

The catalog covers 132 models with the details that decide a local deployment: VRAM requirement at a stated quantization, license, context window, and what the model is actually good at. Around it sit a model recommender, a stack builder, a workstation planner, a token simulator, a quantization guide, a community data view, and a getting-started path.

Who operates this site

Local AI Models is operated by Lagrangian Strategic Partners LLC, an independent engineering practice. The site is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or funded by any model developer, GPU manufacturer, cloud provider, or framework vendor mentioned anywhere on it.

That independence is why the site exists in its current form. Most writing about running models locally comes from a vendor with a product to sell or a publisher optimizing for volume. Neither has much incentive to tell you that the model you are excited about will not fit on your card, or that the benchmark score you are reading does not predict how the model will feel at your context length.

How the data is sourced

The site runs on two kinds of input, and it is worth being precise about which is which, because they carry different confidence.

Automated public signals, refreshed weekly. A scheduled job re-reads a small set of public, freely available sources and folds the results into the catalog and the community data page:

  • HuggingFace Hub, for rolling 30-day download counts and community likes per model repository. The broadest usage picture available under one consistent scheme, because nearly every model in the catalog is published there.
  • Hacker News, through the public Algolia search API, for discussion volume and threads. The fastest available read on what practitioners are actively arguing about, often days ahead of any movement in download counts.
  • arXiv, for related research activity, which separates a model with genuine technical momentum from one having a good publicity week.

Hand-curated catalog entries. Every model card is written and maintained by hand. VRAM figures, license terms, context windows, strengths, weaknesses, and install commands are researched and entered by a person, checked against the model's published documentation, and revised when the model or its ecosystem changes. No part of the catalog is machine-generated. That is slower than automating it, and it is why the catalog holds 132 models rather than a thousand.

What the site does not do

Stating the negative space is as useful as stating the method.

  • No scraped or synthetic reviews. There are no user reviews, star ratings, or aggregate rating markup anywhere on this site. Popularity signals are download counts and likes reported by their source platform, presented as what they are.
  • No affiliate placements. No link on this site earns a commission. Hardware named on the builder and stacks pages is named because it is the relevant hardware, and links go to documentation rather than to storefronts with a tracking parameter attached.
  • No sponsored listings and no paid placement. A model's inclusion, its position in the catalog, and how it is described are editorial decisions. There is no way to buy a listing, a ranking, or a favorable description, and no such arrangement has ever existed.
  • No accounts, no signup, no login. Nothing here requires an account, because nothing here needs to know who you are.

How often it is updated

Honestly stated, because two clocks are running.

The automated signal refresh runs weekly. Download counts, likes, and discussion figures on the model cards and the community page are the values from the most recent run, and they hold steady between runs, so a figure you note on Monday still matches what the page showed that week.

Editorial changes happen when they happen. New models are added when they land and are worth adding, guides are revised when the underlying reality moves, and corrections go out as soon as they are confirmed. That cadence is irregular by nature, and every change is dated on the Updates page, which is the authoritative record of what changed and when. If a page's substance has not moved in a while, the Updates timeline shows that rather than hiding it.

Corrections

Wrong information on a hardware reference costs the reader money, so corrections are treated as a first-class output rather than a quiet edit.

The policy is simple. When a published figure turns out to be wrong, it is fixed on the page and the change is logged as a dated entry on the Updates page, describing what the figure was and what it is now. The old value is not silently overwritten, because a reader who acted on the old number deserves to know it moved.

A worked example, from July 21, 2026. The Recommended Stacks page carried a throughput estimate for 70B models on an M3 Max of roughly 15 to 25 tokens per second. That estimate was too optimistic: it did not reflect what memory bandwidth actually permits on that hardware. It was revised down to roughly 6 to 10 tokens per second, and the correction was published on the Updates page with the previous figure named. That entry is still there and always will be.

If you find something wrong, tell us. Specificity helps: the page, the figure, and either what you measured or where the correct value is published.

How this site is funded

Local AI Models is supported by advertising through Google AdSense. Advertising is what keeps the catalog and the tools free to use, with no paywall and no subscription. It has no influence on editorial content: advertisers do not see content before it publishes, cannot request changes, and cannot buy placement in the catalog.

You stay in control of optional advertising cookies and can accept or reject them through the site's privacy choices at any time. For the full picture of what is collected and how, see the Privacy Policy and the Cookie Policy.

How to reach us

Corrections, model submissions, licensing questions, accessibility issues, and privacy concerns all go to the same place. See the contact page for what to send and where, or write directly to admin@lagrangiansp.com.
