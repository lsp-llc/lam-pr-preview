Methodology · How the data is kept fresh

How this site stays current

Open models ship, get superseded, and fall out of favor on a timescale measured in weeks. A reference that never changed would be wrong within a month. To keep pace without hand-editing every figure, the site pulls a small set of public, freely available signals on a regular schedule and folds the results back into the model catalog and the community data page. This section explains where those numbers come from, what each one actually measures, and where each one can mislead you.

Cadence

Refreshed on a weekly rhythm

An automated job runs on a weekly schedule, re-reads the public sources below, and updates the popularity figures shown on each model card and on the community page. Between runs the numbers hold steady, so a figure you note on Monday still matches what the page showed that week. Last signal refresh: August 24, 2026.

Source

HuggingFace Hub

The Hub is where most open-weight models are published, versioned, and downloaded. It exposes two public counters per repository: a rolling 30-day download count and community likes. These anchor the site's usage picture because they cover nearly every model in the catalog under one consistent scheme.

Source

Hacker News

Hacker News surfaces what practitioners are actively arguing about. When a model launches or a technique catches on, the discussion volume and comment threads there are a fast, public read on developer attention, often days ahead of any download surge.

Source

arXiv

arXiv is where the underlying research is posted. Tracking related papers and their uptake gives a sense of whether a model or method has genuine research momentum behind it, rather than a one-week publicity spike that fades.

Signal · limits

Downloads measure reach, but inflate easily

A download count is the broadest usage proxy available, yet it counts machines, not people. Continuous-integration pipelines, mirrors, re-pulls after a cache clear, and automated evaluation runs all register as downloads. Read it as an order-of-magnitude signal of reach, not a precise headcount of users.

Signal · limits

Likes move slower and endorse

A like is a deliberate act by a logged-in account, so it is harder to inflate and closer to a genuine endorsement. The tradeoff is lag: likes accumulate slowly and undercount models that are heavily used in production but rarely bookmarked. A high like-to-download ratio hints at affection; a low one hints at utility without loyalty.

Signal · limits

Discussion and papers show attention, not quality

Hacker News threads and arXiv activity capture interest and research energy, which often lead the usage curve. But attention is not the same as fitness for your task. A model can dominate discussion for a week and still be the wrong pick for your VRAM budget or license constraints.

Why it matters

A changelog is the honest record

In a field where the leaderboard can turn over in a month, a dated changelog is what separates a current recommendation from a stale one. Every entry above is timestamped so you can see how fresh the underlying view is and weigh it accordingly.

None of these signals is a verdict on its own; the site's job is to place them side by side so you can triangulate. To see the live figures and a fuller how-to-read guide, visit the community data page. To act on them, the model catalog lets you filter by the constraints that actually decide a local deployment (VRAM, license, and modality), and the recommender turns your hardware into a shortlist.

Frequently asked

How often is the data updated?

On a weekly cadence. An automated job re-reads the public sources once a week and refreshes the popularity figures across the catalog and the community page. Last signal refresh: August 24, 2026. Separately, new model additions and site features land as they are ready and are logged in the timeline above, each with its own date and tag.

Where does the data come from?

Three public, freely accessible sources: the HuggingFace Hub for per-model download and like counts, Hacker News for developer discussion, and arXiv for related research activity. Nothing here is pulled from private dashboards or paywalled reports; every figure traces back to a source you can open yourself. The live values and a longer explanation live on the community data page.

Why do the numbers change?

Because the underlying signals are living. The download counter reflects a rolling 30-day window of machine pulls; likes tick up as more accounts endorse it; discussion and paper activity rise and fall with attention. Each weekly refresh captures a new reading, so week-to-week movement is expected. Watching that movement over time is the entire point of tracking it rather than freezing a single snapshot.

How do I read popularity signals?

Treat them as complementary, not interchangeable. Downloads show reach but include automated pulls; likes show slower, more deliberate endorsement; discussion and papers show attention and research momentum that often leads the usage curve. No single number decides whether a model fits your hardware or license needs, so cross-check against the catalog and read the full how-to-read guide on the community data page.
